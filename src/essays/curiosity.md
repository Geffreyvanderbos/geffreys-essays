---
title: Manifesto of curiosity
date: 2026-01-12
---

- I will not participate in cognitive offloading. I'll not give in to the tendency to rely on external tools to reduce mental effort. I won't let it take the drive to seek answers for myself.
- I won't fall victim to digital amnesia. I won't rely on technology to simply store information. I will cultivate understanding and not simply know where to find it.
- Knowledge is best built through active experience and reflection. I won't let technology shortcut my understanding by providing pre-packaged conclusions.
- My focus will be on intrinsic motivation. I enjoy learning for the joy of discovery. I will 'struggle' and be sidetracked to obtain the finished result and not let AI take it from me.
- I will not use technology to solely provide me with novel stimuli to relieve boredom. I will cultivate a deep desire to fill in gaps in knowledge.
- I won't fall for the false sense of mastery that happens when technology generates complex answers effortlessly, making me feel I understand a topic without actually engaging with the material.
- I will accept the human aversion to ambiguity, which technology wants to satisfy instantly. I strive for the state of open-minded exploration.
- I will avoid the elimination of accidental discoveries and tangential learning when doing manual research.
- I will not rely on the technology black box, where I do not know how it reaches its conclusions. My curiosity about the underlying logic, reasoning, and mechanics of the solution will prevail.
- I am wary about algorithmic narrowing: where technology and predictive models limit the scope to only the most probable answers.
- I will not fall for the instant gratification loops: the reinforcement provided by immediate AI rewards, which conditions the brain to prefer quick answers over the slow.
- I won't fear the blank canvas. The idea that starting a task from zero forces creative curiosity, whereas AI auto-complete features fill that gap immediately, stifling the initial spark of invention.
- My mental models of the world are what makes me me.
